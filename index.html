Corporate Group Program Guide

Team building on the water. Up to 24 participants or run in shifts.

Viewing as:
📋Program Overview ▼

Corporate groups are team-building experiences on the water. The goal is not sailing instruction — it is connection, laughter, and a shared challenge. These clients are paying for an experience. Professionalism, energy, and hospitality matter as much as the sailing itself.

🎯 Goal

Fun, memorable, safe. Team bonds on the water. Every participant leaves with a story.

⏱️ Format

3 hours. Up to 24 participants. Larger groups run in shifts — office confirms format before the day.

👥 Group dynamics

Mixed ability, mixed enthusiasm. Some will be excited — some won't want to be there. Your job is to bring both groups along.

📋Know Your Group Before They Arrive ▼

Corporate groups vary more than any other program. Check with the office before the session — they have the booking details and any client requests.

Questions to ask admin before the session

  • How many participants and are there any shifts?
  • Is there a key contact person from the company — who is it?
  • Any specific requests from the client — races, photos, specific activities?
  • Any participants with mobility issues, injuries, or concerns flagged?
  • Waivers — all signed before arrival or collect on the day?
  • Any catering, setup, or branding requests?

Common group types

⚡ High energy — here to compete

Want races, challenges, and a winner. Run a structured regatta-style session. Keep score. Announce results at the debrief.

🤝 Team building — here to connect

Want collaboration and shared experience. Pair people who don't usually work together. Focus on communication drills and follow-the-leader.

🎉 Social — here to have fun

No agenda beyond a good time. Keep it light, encourage laughs, and make sure everyone feels included. Capsize recovery can be a highlight — frame it as fun, not scary.

😐 Mixed enthusiasm

Someone didn't choose to be here. Find them early, get them in a boat quickly, give them a small win. Most reluctant participants come around — meet them where they are.

💡
The reluctant participant: Don't single them out or push. Pair them with an enthusiastic colleague. Put a coach in their boat. Give them something they can succeed at quickly. Most will come around — but if they genuinely don't want to be on the water, don't force it. Let them watch and stay involved on shore.

Roles at a glance

👤 Lead Instructor

  • Liaises with client contact on the day
  • Runs the land briefing — high energy, welcoming
  • Oversees launch and on-water session
  • Manages any reluctant participants
  • Calls contingencies
  • Leads group debrief and wrap-up
  • Logs the session after

🏖️ Beach Manager

  • Gear ready before group arrives
  • Welcoming presence at arrival — first face they see
  • PFD and wetsuit fitting
  • Manages launch zone
  • Radio monitor on shore
  • Gear return and lost & found

⛵ Support Instructor

  • Rig boats before group arrives
  • Help participants gear up — friendly and warm
  • On-water coaching — keep it encouraging
  • Run activities and drills as directed by lead
  • Guide boats back in
  • Assist de-rig

🚢 Hobie Coach

  • Sail with up to 4 participants — coach in the boat
  • Ideal for less confident or VIP participants
  • Keep the energy positive and social
  • Radio on — respond when lead calls
🔧Pre-Arrival Setup (30 min before) ▼

👤 Lead Instructor

  • Confirm group size, format, and any special requests with admin
  • Check wind and weather — have contingency plan ready
  • Brief all support staff — roles, activities planned, client context
  • Know the client contact name before they arrive
  • Set the course — make it visible and well-positioned
  • Have activities planned: follow the leader, relay race, regatta format

⛵ Support Instructor

  • Rig boats — no jib on Zests
  • Check plugs, rudders, and safety gear
  • Stage boats neatly — presentation matters for corporate
  • Area looking clean and professional before group arrives
  • Report any gear issues to lead immediately

🏖️ Beach Manager

  • Wetsuits organized and gear station ready
  • Life jackets opened and ready
  • Classroom set up — clean, chairs ready, whiteboard if needed
  • Site looking sharp — corporate clients notice presentation
  • Confirm waivers with office
🏢
Presentation standard: Corporate clients see MacSailing as a reflection of the experience they're providing their team. The site, gear, and staff should look sharp from the moment they arrive. First impressions set the tone for the whole session.
⛵Session Timeline — 3 Hours ▼
0:00–0:30
Briefing

Welcome & Land Instruction

👤 Lead runs the briefing — high energy, warm, confident

  • Welcome (3 min) — introduce yourself and the team by name. Acknowledge the company. "Welcome to MacSailing — we're going to get you on the water today."
  • Safety (5 min) — lifejackets always on near water, capsize is normal and recoverable, who to call if you need help
  • Boat parts (5 min) — keep it light. Tiller like a microphone, flat sail goes, flappy sail slows. Don't overload them with theory.
  • Tacking (5 min) — demo on a boat on shore. Run through the steps once together. Keep it simple.
  • The plan (2 min) — tell them what's happening. "We'll spend about 75 minutes on the water — you'll be surprised how quickly you get it."
  • Energy check — read the room. If they're nervous, reassure. If they're pumped, match it.

⛵ Support during briefing

  • Launch coach boats
  • Final boat checks
  • Be visible and welcoming — smile, introduce yourself to participants near you
  • Help with any late arrivals quietly
0:30–1:00
Gear Up

Gear Up, Grouping & Launch Prep

👤 Lead

  • Monitor groupings — mix teams across departments where possible
  • Identify any reluctant participants — assign a support instructor or Hobie
  • Quick land tacking practice — 2–3 minutes, keep it fun

⛵ Support / Beach Manager

  • Help with PFDs and wetsuits — keep it efficient and friendly
  • Pair participants into boats — balanced energy levels
  • Keep the atmosphere warm — chat, make them feel welcome
1:00–2:15
On Water

On-Water Session — 75 min

👤 Lead Instructor

  • Launch in waves of 2–4 boats — same as school groups
  • Scale back immediately if wind exceeds 10 knots
  • Run structured activities — rotate based on group energy
  • Keep the energy high — commentate, encourage, celebrate moments
  • Radio communication with beach throughout

⛵ Support Instructor

  • Stay with your assigned boats
  • Run the activity as directed by lead
  • Give encouragement constantly — "Yes! That's it!" works for adults too
  • Keep it fun — laugh with them, not at them
  • Radio check-ins with lead regularly

2:15–2:40
Return

Return to Shore & De-rig — 25 min

👤 Lead

  • Call all boats in via radio
  • Keep the energy positive as they return
  • Ensure boats secured and gear returned neatly

⛵ Support

  • Guide boats in 1–2 at a time
  • Help de-rig — keep it efficient
  • Keep talking to participants — "How was that? What was your best moment?"
2:40–3:00
Wrap Up

Group Debrief & Farewell — 20 min

👤 Lead runs the wrap-up

  • Bring the whole group together — high energy finish
  • If you ran a regatta or relay: announce results, celebrate the winners
  • Ask: "What was the highlight of the day for you?"
  • Acknowledge the company organizer by name — thank them for bringing the team
  • Tell them about Mac Crew and courses — "If anyone wants to come back..."
  • Professional farewell — you're the last impression they have of MacSailing

⛵ Support during wrap-up

  • Engage warmly with participants
  • Help organize belongings and gear returns
  • Clear lost & found
  • Keep the positive energy going until the last person leaves
🌟
Last impression: The way the session ends is what they remember and what they tell their colleagues. Make the farewell warm, specific, and memorable.
🔧Running Shifts (Groups over 24) ▼

For groups larger than 24, the session runs in shifts. The office will confirm the format before the day — confirm with admin how many shifts and how long each is.

Between shifts

  • Quick team reset — brief your support staff on any changes
  • Check gear is back in place and boats are ready
  • Check in with the client contact — anything to adjust?
  • Fresh energy for the new group — every shift gets the same experience

Lead Instructor

Support Instructor

  • You set the pace across all shifts
  • Brief each new group as if it's the first — same energy
  • Keep notes on any issues between shifts
  • Single log after the last shift covers all groups
  • Reset your gear and boats between shifts
  • Same energy for every group — don't let it drop by shift 3
  • Flag anything to the lead between shifts, not during
🚨Contingency Plans ▼

🌬️ Heavy Wind (10+ knots)

  • Reduce boats on water
  • Hobie rides for those waiting on shore
  • Beach station activities — knots, boat parts, photo ops
  • Lead makes the call — never compromise safety for a client

🍃 Light / No Wind

  • Still launch — paddling is part of sailing
  • Follow the leader behind a coach boat
  • Relay races work in light wind
  • Frame it positively — "Perfect conditions for learning"

😤 Difficult Participant

  • Stay calm and professional at all times
  • Don't engage in conflict on the water or in front of the group
  • Pass to manager if the situation escalates
  • Log it after — never address a client complaint in the moment
⚠️
Client complaints: Stay calm → listen briefly → acknowledge → pass to manager. Never argue, never over-promise, never make commitments on behalf of MacSailing. Log it and let the manager handle the follow-up.
